## Faults found, and what was done

1. **Validator-5 was flooded with `owned_apply_v3`.** Its key was rotated after the FastPay committee was recorded, so it holds a certified payment without signing and answers with an unsigned held-effect receipt. The wallet proxy read that as a failed delivery and resent the certificate every two seconds. Three certificates from about block 1036 had been cycling since at least 22 October: 4.1 million calls in validator-5's RPC logs.
   - **Fix:** #239, merged. A held-effect receipt that names the validator and the certificate's lock ends the delivery. It never counts toward the signed quorum.
   - **The proxy doing it runs on the machine that logs in with the `postfiat-cobalt-shadow-20260823` key.** I could not log in there, and I have not confirmed whose it is. On validator-5 I prefixed that key's line with `permitopen="127.0.0.1:9"` so it can still log in but cannot forward to the RPC, and dropped its session. The earlier file is `/root/authorized_keys.before-20261125-flood`.
   - **To lift the block:** deploy a proxy build that has #239 on that host, then restore that file. Check `rpc-events.ndjson` shows no repeated `owned_apply_v3` for one certificate over an hour.
2. **RPC reads fail while a block is committed.** redb gives the database file to one process; a read that arrives during a commit waited the 20 second lease and failed with `storage_writer_busy`. This stopped the B24 reading after its finalize step. #243 answers `status` from the report read before the commit, for at most 30 seconds, after a 2 second wait. #252 (not mine, on `main`, not deployed) covers receipts. Issue #241 has the mechanism.
3. **The B24 job stopped on one failed status read.** #238 retries `rpc_status_failed`. The epoch 10 reading was finished by hand: route pricing run alone, then all three steps confirmed by receipt on six validators.
4. **A started-early scheduled reading still measures at 18:15 UTC.** "Now" means `daily.py --now`. I started the scheduled service early and gained nothing.
5. **The rehearsal tool's hash is per machine.** The hash you listed in #235 was the work server's. On the relay host the same build gives another, twice running. `known-releases.txt` lists the relay host's and says so.
6. **Service output logs on the validators were never rotated.** `rpc-stdout.log` was 0.5 to 1 GB per host and validator-0 was at 88% disk. #240 records what I installed on all six: rotation at 100 MB, compressed event logs, journald capped at 1 GB. No validator unit was restarted. The 4 GB `transport-validator-events.ndjson.pre-rotation-20260929.zst` on five hosts is left for the owner.
7. **The relay host's root disk reached 99%.** I removed about 72 GB of Rust `target` folders and unused Docker images. `~/var/pq-work` was not touched. The list is in `/tmp/removed-targets.txt`. Nothing prunes these automatically.
8. **PR #232's head did not build a node binary.** `postfiat-privacy-binius` calls the spend-authorization check, which the tool implements under its `prover` feature; only test builds enabled it, so every hosted test passed. I enabled it in the normal dependency and pushed that to the branch. A replay with that build matched the running release byte for byte. Several hosted checks failed on that push and I have not looked at why. #244 (not mine) now builds the release binary in CI.

## Proving on Colab

The two Vast provers cost $3.34 an hour for a handful of proofs a day. All four proof types now run on one Colab GPU session.

| Proof, warm | Colab G4 | Vast |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Solana deposit | 66 to 73 s | 62 s |
| Withdrawal (Solana or Ethereum) | 23 to 41 s | 122 s |
| B24 | 155 s on an A100; not timed on a G4 | 135 s |

- **G4** is an RTX PRO 6000 with 96 GB and 48 cores. Deposit proving is limited by CPU, not GPU: on the 12-core A100 it took 253 s with the GPU at 44%.
- **A real 0.30 USDC deposit** (`46kdr5ET…`) was proved on the session in 73 s and credited on six validators 123 s after sending.
- **How the services reach it.** They keep their commands. While a window is open, `~/.ssh/config.d/colab-prover.conf` sends `ssh8.vast.ai` through one multiplexed connection to the session (Colab allows one SSH connection per runtime). The deposit prover's socket and the withdrawal door's port are forwarded over the same connection.
- **The Solana withdrawal service** runs as its own user, so it needed root: `/etc/hosts` now points `ssh8.vast.ai` at `127.0.0.1`, and `/var/lib/pf-sol-exit/known_hosts` holds the session's fixed host key (earlier file beside it, `.vast-20261126`). On the session a second SSH daemon admits only that service's key with its forced command. `sudo ~/colab-sol-withdraw.sh revert` undoes both.
- **Window:** session at 16:15, open 17:00 to 19:00 UTC, so that it covers the 18:15 B24 reading. Timers `colab-prover-open`, `-close` and `-ensure` are installed and not enabled.
- **Everything is in `~/var/colab-prover`** and recorded in #257. The prover binaries (506 MB) and the session's keys stay on the relay host.

What went wrong while building it, so you do not repeat it:

- The first unattended open failed. No G4 was free, the fallback A100 was fine, and then the circuit download timed out: the bucket gives one connection about 5 MB/s. The launcher now fetches with 16 ranges against a pinned hash (3 min 43 s).
- A second GPU of the same type is sometimes refused as unavailable. Two sessions at once are allowed.
- On a 40 GB GPU the warm deposit prover and a withdrawal proof do not fit together. The launcher keeps deposits closed for that window if it gets one.
- Vast binaries need `/usr/lib64-nvidia` and the CUDA 12 runtime on the loader path, and `sp1-gpu-server` needs `CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES`. `provision.sh` does both.
